# Merox Chemistry Cheatsheet

## Extraction stage

    RSH + NaOH → RS-Na + H2O

Equilibrium-limited; RSH distribution between LPG and aqueous caustic
depends on caustic concentration, T, and the RSH chain length. Lighter
mercaptans (MeSH, EtSH) extract more readily than C4+ mercaptans.

## Regeneration (oxidiser)

    2 RS-Na + 0.5 O2 + H2O → RSSR + 2 NaOH

Cobalt phthalocyanine catalyst (or successor catalysts). The disulfide
product separates from the caustic in a gravity / coalescer separator
("Stelter" or downstream sand filter). Poor disulfide removal causes
caustic disulfide to climb; some disulfide carries back to the extractor
and partitions into the LPG.

## Free vs total alkalinity

- Total alkalinity = unreacted NaOH + RS-Na + Na2CO3 (if CO2 slip) +
  other sodium salts.
- Free alkalinity = unreacted NaOH (typically titrated with strong acid to
  the phenolphthalein endpoint).
- Bound alkalinity = Total − Free.

Mercaptide loading reduces free alkalinity; for steady extraction at
constant RSH load and constant regeneration, free alkalinity is the
relevant figure of merit.

## Common failure modes

- **Regeneration lagging**: free alkalinity drops, RS-Na loading climbs,
  RSH slip rises.
- **Disulfide carryback**: poor separator performance leaves disulfide in
  lean caustic; it returns to the extractor and partitions to the LPG.
- **Sodium sulfide formation** (if H2S is not removed upstream of Merox):
  caustic accumulates Na2S, which is less easily regenerated and leads to
  caustic colour shift.
- **Foaming**: from amine carryover or surfactant contamination; flagged
  by extractor dP rise.
