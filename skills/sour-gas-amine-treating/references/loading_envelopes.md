# Rich Loading Envelopes (Public Literature)

Rich loading caps are widely cited in the open literature (Kohl & Nielsen,
GPSA Engineering Data Book, vendor handbooks). They are guides only;
material-of-construction, amine concentration, and inhibitor presence shift
the safe envelope. Cross-check with your licensed technology provider.

| Amine | Typical rich loading cap (mol acid / mol amine) | Comment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MEA | 0.35-0.40 | Aggressive; corrosion risk drives the cap. |
| DEA | 0.40-0.45 | Common in refining; relatively benign. |
| MDEA | 0.45-0.55 | Higher capacity; selective for H2S. |
| DGA | 0.45-0.50 | Higher boiling, less degradation. |
| Piperazine (blend) | varies | Used as MDEA activator; depends on blend ratio. |

## Concentration interaction

Higher amine wt% generally reduces the allowable rich loading because of
faster corrosion. Typical operating ranges:

- MEA: 15-20 wt%.
- DEA: 25-35 wt% (refinery); some service up to 40 wt%.
- MDEA: 35-50 wt% (gas processing).
- DGA: 50-65 wt%.

## H2S vs CO2 selectivity

- DEA and MEA: both H2S and CO2 absorbed similarly (non-selective).
- MDEA: prefers H2S; CO2 absorption is rate-limited (slip CO2 is common).
- Mixed acid gases need explicit speciation: report H2S loading and CO2
  loading separately when feed has both.

## Notes

- These are screening envelopes; vendor packages (Coastal AGR, Sulferox,
  BASF aMDEA, etc.) have their own envelopes that may differ.
- Operation outside the envelope is sometimes done with inhibitors and
  enhanced monitoring; do not exceed without licensor concurrence.
